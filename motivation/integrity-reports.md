# Integrity Reports and Guidelines Paper

The papers herein included denounce cases of possible scientific misconduct or any other related issues.

They are organized by media type (*e.g., text or image*) and chronological sorted within the media types.

The articles are sorted by date, recent papers should appear at the earlier than old papers.



## Images

**Sep 2022**- [Blind spots on western blots: Assessment of common problems in western blot figures and methods reporting with recommendations to improve them](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3001783) - Kronn, C. et al.

- Systematically examined 551 articles published in the top 25% of journals in neurosciences (n = 151) and cell biology (n = 400) that contained western blot images, focusing on practices that may omit important information. The data show that most published western blots are cropped, and blot source data are not made available to readers in the supplement.

**Mar 2019** [Figure errors, sloppy science, and fraud: keeping eyes on your data](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/128380) - Williams C.L., Casadevall A., and Jackson S.

- Introduced several data screening checks for manuscripts prior to acceptance in an attempt to reduce the number of post-publication corrections and retractions, with the ultimate goal of increasing confidence in the papers we publish.

**Jun 2018** - [Analysis and Correction of Inappropriate Image Duplication: the Molecular and Cellular Biology Experience](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1128/MCB.00309-18)- Bik E., Fang F.C., Kullas A.L., Davis R.J., and Casadevall A.

- MCB instituted a pilot program to screen images of accepted papers prior to publication that identified 12 manuscripts (14.5% out of 83) with image concerns in 2 months. The screening and correction of papers before publication required an average of 30 min of staff time per problematic paper

**June 2016** - [The Prevalence of Inappropriate Image Duplication in Biomedical Research Publications](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mbio.00809-16) - Bik, E., Casadevall, A.,  and Fang F.C. (:fire::fire::fire:)

- This is a game-changing article. The impact of this article made people take more serious all previous allegations, by acknowledging that image misconduct is not an isolated issue.
- The images from **20,621 papers published in 40 scientific journals from 1995 to 2014** were visually screened. **3.8% of published papers contained problematic figures, with at least half exhibiting features suggestive of deliberate manipulation.**

**Jan 2021** - [Memorandum on Restoring Trust in Government Through Scientific Integrity and Evidence-Based Policymaking](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/presidential-actions/2021/01/27/memorandum-on-restoring-trust-in-government-through-scientific-integrity-and-evidence-based-policymaking/) - The White House of U.S.A

- Agencies must adopt robust policies to safeguard the integrity of data, preventing distortion or suppression of scientific findings.
- Agencies are directed to align their processes with evidence-based practices to enhance transparency and trust in government actions.

**Oct 2020** - [Misinformation in and about science](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1912444117) - West J.D., and Bergstrom C.T. (:fire::fire:)

- Most analyses of misinformation focus on popular and social media, but the scientific enterprise faces a parallel set of problems—from hype and hyperbole to publication bias and citation misdirection, predatory publishing, and filter bubbles.
- Highlight these problems and discuss future research directions and interventions.

**July 2020** - [Collaboration and its influence on retraction based on retracted publications during 1978–2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-020-03636-w) - Zhang Q., Abraham J., and Fu H.Z. 

-  The USA and China contributed to more than half of all retracted publications, while retraction rate of Chinese papers was three times higher compared to papers from the USA.

- Countries involved in more international collaborations tend to have lower retraction rates.
- The occurrences of plagiarism was found to be lower when more authors contribute a paper, while falsification or manipulation, errors or unreliable results appeared more frequently under the same condition.

 **Mar 2020** - [Retraction: The “Other Face” of Research Collaboration?](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-020-00209-1) - Tang Li., Hu G., Sui Y., Yang Y., and Cao C.

- Investigate factors contributing to retraction probability and elapsed time between publication and retraction.

-  Publications with authors from elite universities are less likely to be retracted, which is particularly true for retractions due to falsification, fabrication, and plagiarism. China stands out with the fastest retracting speed compared to other countries.

**Jan 2020** - [Gaming the Metrics: Misconduct and Manipulation in Academic Research](https://direct.mit.edu/books/oa-edited-volume/4598/Gaming-the-MetricsMisconduct-and-Manipulation-in) Biagioli M., Lippman A. (​ :book: ​)

 **Sep 2017** - [Lack of Improvement in Scientific Integrity: An Analysis of WoS Retractions by Chinese Researchers (1997–2016)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-017-9962-7) - Lei L., and Zhang Y.

- A large proportion of the retractions seemed typical of deliberate fraud, which might be evidenced by retractions authored by repeat offenders of data fraud and those due to faked peer review.
